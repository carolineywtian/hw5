DESIGN NOTE — how cash is decided

Tickets can run in any order and get the same money decisions. The team decides with available cash, not the bank balance:

available cash = $3,400 cash − every known bill (invoice 501 $840 + rent $2,400, queued or not, counted once) − pending purchase orders = $160

  • Why: in a first version, the cash check only subtracted payments already queued. Testing all 6 run orders showed the 12-hoodie restock ($264) looked affordable whenever ticket 103 ran before the rent or invoice was queued, leaving the shop $104 short in 4 of 6 orders, sometimes on rent. It only worked when 103 ran last.
  • Fix (real-life accounting): a bill counts as soon as the shop knows about it. Payment priority is rent → overdue invoices → invoices not yet due → new purchase orders. The MCP server refuses a purchase order that doesn't fit after the known bills. Rent and invoices are never refused; if they don't fit, the Boss escalates. Low-cash warning below $500.
  • Re-tested: all 6 orders now give $160 available, the restock is refused by the server, and the low-cash warning is on, every time.

3. What would have been simpler as 1 agent with tools, and why

  • Ticket 102 (Elm City rent) needed no team. The team used 13 tool calls and 11 model requests; three agents each called get_ticket(102), and Facilities and Accounting both called get_cash_and_bills. One "shop clerk" agent could do it in 5 calls: get_ticket(102) → get_lease(1) → get_cash_and_bills() → create_approval_request("rent_payment", ref_id=1) → update_ticket(102, "waiting_approval"). Same $2,400 request #2, about a third of the calls, and nothing to coordinate. Ticket 102 → tools
  • Ticket 101 (Tauhid's tee) would have been better, not just cheaper, as one agent. One agent calling check_stock_and_price("CC-TEE-WHITE") would see S: 0, M: 5 and write the draft to Tauhid in the same breath, so the size-M offer can't get lost between agents the way it did at 11:04:19. About 6 calls (get_ticket, check_stock_and_price, get_vendor(1), create_approval_request("invoice_payment", ref_id=501), save_customer_message, update_ticket) instead of 17. Ticket 101 → tools
  • Ticket 103 (Yale AI Club) is where the team earned its cost. A single agent would very likely have queued the $394.40 override and stopped. It was the Boss reviewing Accounting's request #3 that spotted the 8-vs-10-unit conflict and escalated it to me. Splitting roles also kept money safe: only Accounting could queue #1, #2 and #3, only the Boss could set 103 to escalated, and no agent could approve anything.
  • So for Campus Customs I'd route by ticket type. Rent notices and one-item customer orders go to one agent with read tools plus create_approval_request and save_customer_message. Discount requests, partial stock and anything competing for our $160 go to the team with Boss review.

My takeaway for Campus Customs

On our three tickets the team got every Campus Customs fact and dollar right: 0 size-S tees in Aisle B, invoice 501 three days overdue, lease 1 due 2026-09-02, 8 hoodies in Aisle A, $160 left.
